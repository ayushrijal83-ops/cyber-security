## <span style="font-size: 24px">What is Base64?</span>

<span style="font-size: 24px">Base64 is an </span>**<span style="font-size: 24px">encoding scheme</span>**<span style="font-size: 24px"> — a way to convert any data (text, binary, images) into a string of 64 safe characters:</span>

- `A-Z`<span style="font-size: 24px"> (26)</span>

- `a-z`<span style="font-size: 24px"> (26)</span>

- `0-9`<span style="font-size: 24px"> (10)</span>

- `+`<span style="font-size: 24px"> and </span>`/`<span style="font-size: 24px"> (2)</span>

<span style="font-size: 24px">It splits data into 3-byte chunks, converts them into 4 groups of 6 bits, and maps each group to one of the 64 characters.</span>

**<span style="font-size: 24px">How to recognize it:</span>**<span style="font-size: 24px"> Base64 strings often end with </span>`=`<span style="font-size: 24px"> or </span>`==`<span style="font-size: 24px"> (padding).</span>

**<span style="font-size: 24px">Important:</span>**<span style="font-size: 24px"> Base64 is </span>**<span style="font-size: 24px">NOT encryption</span>**<span style="font-size: 24px">. It's just a format change. Anyone can decode it instantly — no key required.</span>

## <span style="font-size: 24px">The </span>`echo -n`<span style="font-size: 24px"> flag</span>

<span style="font-size: 24px">By default, </span>`echo`<span style="font-size: 24px"> adds a newline (</span>`\n`<span style="font-size: 24px">) at the end of output. The </span>`-n`<span style="font-size: 24px"> flag prevents that.</span>

<span style="font-size: 24px">Why it matters: if you encode </span>`"hello\n"`<span style="font-size: 24px"> instead of </span>`"hello"`<span style="font-size: 24px">, you get a different Base64 result. </span>`-n`<span style="font-size: 24px"> ensures you encode exactly the string you intended.</span>
